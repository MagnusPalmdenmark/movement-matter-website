Our mission

Made of what matters.

Most activewear is plastic. We wear it tight against the skin, for hours, while we sweat — and almost nobody reads the label.

Movement Matter makes activewear mostly from trees, not oil. Fewer pieces, in black, released in small drops — and honest about what’s in them.

01 — The problem

Most activewear is plastic.

139 million

59%

02 — Recycled is still plastic

A bottle can become a bottle again. A legging can’t.

Recycled polyester sounds like the answer. But it’s still polyester: it still sheds plastic fibres in the wash, and almost all of it is made from bottles, not old clothes.

Less than 1% of the world’s fibre comes from recycled textiles. Blends with stretch fibres are especially hard to recycle again. Once a bottle is turned into fibre, that loop is broken.

Textile Exchange, Materials Market Report 2025
03 — What we make instead

94% wood. 6% stretch.

Our fabric is 94% wood-based viscose and 6% elastane. The viscose comes from trees. The elastane gives the stretch — it is plastic, and we say so.

  • FibreLENZING™ ECOVERO™ viscose
  • FootprintAt least 50% less CO₂ and water than generic viscose
  • WoodFSC® or PEFC certified sources
  • LabelEU Ecolabel, the EU’s official environmental label

We test before we promise. Stretch recovery, pilling and breathability are measured on our own fabric — results published here when they’re in.

Fibre data: Lenzing AG
04 — Read the label

Plastic has many names.

PolyesterNylonPolyamideElastaneSpandexLycraAcrylic

The biggest number on the label is what you’re mostly wearing. Look for fibres made from plants and wood instead: cotton, linen, hemp, viscose, modal, lyocell.
